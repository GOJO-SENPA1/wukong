How it stays honest

A model is one witness. Wukong is the court.

01

Facts are checked by code

Word limits, required and banned phrases, language and script, line structure, the code's language. Code measures them exactly; the model is never asked to count.

02

Quote it or it doesn't count

Every score cites the entry word for word. Code checks each quote, and a verdict that invents one is sent back. An invented quote has never been published.

03

Three verdicts, then the median

Each entry is judged three times independently. The median score wins, flags need a majority, and if the verdicts disagree, more are drawn.

04

Rules no model can argue with

Break a hard requirement and the entry is capped at 60. Fail the main criterion and it's capped at 50. Try to manipulate the judge and it scores 0.

05

A two-layer injection shield

A scanner that sees through look-alike letters, invisible characters, base64 and other languages, plus a judge that treats every entry strictly as data.

06

Published on-chain

Every score goes on Solana with a hash of its written report, so anyone can check that what we show is what we decided.

How we measured

Proof, not vibes.

  • Answers we can prove. Code entries are graded by running a real test suite; labeling entries against an answer key; writing and translation around planted, checkable defects (a broken word limit, a wrong breakfast time, an allergy warning softened, invented statistics).
  • Five kinds of work: copywriting, translation into Nepali and Spanish, JavaScript, sentiment labeling and research, plus the attack suite.
  • Three full runs. Ranking accuracy is the average of all three (97.3%, 96.2%, 95.9%); the other Wukong figures are from the first run.
  • The baseline is the same fast open-weight model with the same prompt and rubric, one request per entry and none of the pipeline.
  • Our own benchmark. We wrote it, and we tuned the judge's instructions on it, so treat the tuned numbers as a best case. Before tuning the Wukong judge already ranked 94.7% of pairs correctly. The held-out attacks were never used for tuning.
  • What it still misses: a sentence-by-sentence paraphrase of someone else's entry, and photo-finish calls between two strong copywriting entries (both misses were decided by under 2 points).
  • Cheap to run. A full pass over all 98 entries, three verdicts each, costs about a third of a dollar.
