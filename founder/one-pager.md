# Wukong: one page

**Problem.** Hiring one person for a small task is a gamble, and picking a winner by hand is slow. Contest sites make workers compete for free with no clear rules. Everyone worries about who holds the money.

**Customer.** Operators who keep a backlog of small, measurable tasks (copy with limits, translation, labeling, small coding jobs), starting with web3 and community organisers and education organisations. Workers: freelancers and students who want a verifiable record.

**Offer and price.** The buyer locks the reward before anyone starts, many people enter, an AI judge scores every entry in public with a written reason, and only the top entries are paid; if nothing clears the bar, the buyer is refunded. Typical task: **30 USDC reward, about 33 USDC all-in**. Wukong keeps a **10% fee ($3.00)**. The first task is free for the first 10 organisations.

**Evidence.** The judge's public benchmark (98 entries, 25 attack attempts): 96.5% of pairs ranked correctly, 25 of 25 attacks caught, 0 of 11 false alarms. Caveat: the team wrote and tuned on the dataset. A panel of 40 simulated buyers: 5 would try a free first task and none would pay (12.5%); on the improved offer, 4 of 20 (20%). The competitor map: nobody in the sources combines automated public scoring, money locked first and any measurable task. October email outreach: 19 replies, about 7 positive, none yet funded a task.

**What the money is for.** An independent security audit (estimate $15,000, quotes pending), forming the company, a mainnet deployment, ten founder-funded pilot tasks (about $300), and 12 months at a modest stipend and hosting.

**The ask.** About **$28,000**, the model's cash-needed figure ($27,655). It is a result built on estimates, not a quote. The first milestone is the pilot test: 3 of 6 warm leads run a task, 2 call the winner usable, 1 agrees to pay for a second, reviewed 21 December 2026.

*The panel is simulated buyers; the numbers are projections. Not financial, legal or tax advice.*
