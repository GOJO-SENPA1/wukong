# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- Buyer side: the value equation is well shaped. Escrow locked before work starts, a public rubric and on-chain scores cut the buyer's effort and risk. Several submissions compete, so the chance of a good result goes up. Pay is only released for quality, so the buyer carries little downside.
- Small, measurable tasks (word-limited copy, tests-backed code, labeling) suit an automated judge. The pain is real but narrow.
- Worker side: free entry, a refundable stake only for spam, and a portable verifiable record. This is a clear hook for students and early-career freelancers who lack proof of skill.
- Some proof of the judge: WukongBench shows 96.5% of pairs ranked correctly, against 90.0% for the bare model, and 25 of 25 attacks caught. The caveats are honest: same-team dataset, tuned on it, paraphrase copies missed.
- About 7 positive replies out of cold outreach shows curiosity, not demand.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Worker economics are poor.** Winner-takes-all or 50/30/20 means most entrants work for free. Two agencies said so bluntly. If the expected pay per hour of entry is low, good workers leave and quality falls. That breaks the likelihood-of-result promise for buyers.
2. **No proof anyone will pay.** No outside organisation has posted a task or locked money. The 7 "send them over" replies are the weakest signal. Devnet, demo USDC, no audit: a buyer cannot lock real funds, and "AI judge decides" has no track record on real, subjective work.
3. **Cold start on both sides, and a market with unproven money.** Buyers need enough entries to get quality fast; workers need enough tasks to bother. The buyer's alternatives (Fiverr-style gigs, one freelancer, an LLM directly) are cheap and quick, so this offer must beat them on outcome or effort, not price. A 10% fee on a 50 USDC task is about 5 USDC, so there is little revenue per task. Trust problems (anonymous sender names, no site in the first email) hurt credibility too.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which named buyer will lock real money on a real task, and when? What is their budget per task and how often?
- What does an entrant earn per hour on average? How many entries per task make a buyer happy, and how many make a worker quit?
- How many tasks does a buyer need to post per month for the fee to matter? What is the open question on fee revenue per customer?
- Why is this better for a buyer than hiring one freelancer or asking a model directly? Is that measured?
- What is the guarantee if the judge ranks badly or no entry is good? Who handles disputes?
- How will you get the first 20 workers per task, and what does each cost?
- What do the college-career-club and community CEO leads actually commit to?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- At least 3 outside organisations post real tasks with real funds (mainnet or a clear test with real payment) and repeat once.
- Show worker earnings data from those tasks: entries per task, and pay per entrant, at a level students would accept (for example via a participation floor or smaller entry pools).
- A written buyer guarantee: refund or re-run if the judge score falls below a stated threshold, plus a human appeal path.
- Independent judge testing on data the team did not write, including the paraphrase-copy gap.
- A narrow first niche (for example student micro-internships or event explainers) with a named, reachable buyer group.
