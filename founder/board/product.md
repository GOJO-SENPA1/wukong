# Product lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The buyer's promise is clear in one sentence: lock the reward, get many sealed entries, pay the best one. Escrow before work starts answers the worker's biggest fear, which is not being paid.
- Public rubric plus a published on-chain score per entry is a distinctive, checkable experience. Workers can see why they lost, which is the answer to "what becomes of the losers?" if it is shown well.
- The scope is already narrow: small, measurable tasks (word limits, tests, labeling). This suits automated judging.
- Craft shows in places users cannot see: 25/25 attacks caught, 0/11 false alarms, 97.4% of scores backed by a verified quote. Caveat: the team wrote the dataset and tuned on it.
- The worker's portable reputation is a real reason to come back.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The first five minutes are unproven on both sides.** Nobody outside has posted a task. Outreach replies are interest, not use. A buyer must pick a rubric, fund USDC and wait. A worker must sign up, understand sealed entries and accept unpaid effort. The brief does not say how long either takes.
2. **The seams.** A buyer arrives from email, then Privy sign-in, then needs USDC in a wallet (a non-crypto buyer may not have one), then escrow, then judge, then payout. Each handoff can lose them. Devnet demo USDC and an unaudited program mean no buyer has yet felt the real money step. The cold emails came from invented sender names with no visible website, and one recipient doubted it was legitimate. The first impression was a product flaw.
3. **Spec work and judge trust.** Two agencies rejected contest work. Workers risk unpaid effort on tasks where a score they cannot argue with decides the outcome. If the judge misses paraphrase copies, or a loser feels cheated, trust breaks on both sides. The product has no stated appeal path.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is the one thing Wukong does brilliantly? Which of Blinks, x402 and reputation would you cut?
- Can a first-time buyer post and fund a task in under five minutes without owning a wallet? Have you watched anyone try?
- Can a first-time worker find a task, understand the rules and submit in five minutes? What do losers receive?
- What happens when the judge is wrong or a buyer disputes the winner?
- How many entries does a typical task get? What is the open question on worker supply per task?
- What is the real fee path in USDC, and who pays for gas?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Run 3 to 5 real tasks with outside organisations, from the interested replies, with timed, observed first sessions for buyer and worker.
- Remove the wallet barrier: card or fiat funding, or a managed wallet, so a buyer never meets a seed phrase.
- Give every loser a visible written reason and a short path to challenge a score.
- Replace the invented sender identities with a real name and a real site.
- Complete an audit, or limit mainnet to small capped escrows, before any real money.
- Report results on an independent task set, not the tuned benchmark.
