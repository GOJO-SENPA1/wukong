# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- Timing is a real argument. AI judges that score against a public rubric, USDC escrow, and AI agents paying over x402 only recently became workable together. The "why now" is credible.
- The one technical asset worth noting: the judging pipeline beat the bare model (96.5% vs 90.0% of pairs ranked correctly), caught 25/25 injection attacks, and backed 97.4% of scores with a verified quote. But the team wrote the dataset and tuned on it, and the judge misses paraphrase copies. It is not yet a 10x lead.
- Escrow before work plus on-chain scores and portable reputation is a coherent trust design. Reputation could become a network effect if workers accumulate it and cannot take it elsewhere. Today it is portable by design, so it is not a lock-in.
- Open source (MIT) and cheap to run, so the burn is low.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No moat, and a copycat takes a weekend.** Everything is MIT-licensed, including the program, the app and the judge pipeline. The judge model is swappable. A funded competitor, or a Web2 contest platform, could fork it in month six and bring its own buyers. Reputation is portable by design, so it does not trap anyone. Nothing here is ten times better in a way that holds up.
2. **No distribution on either side, and the buyer side has refused to commit.** The first 100 buyers have no named channel. After about 3 days of work there is no real task from an outside organisation. About 7 positive replies out of 19 are interest, not money. Two design agencies rejected contest and spec work outright. Buyers whose work needs taste will avoid the format. The workable segments are tasks with objective, measurable rubrics: copy with word limits, code with tests, labeling. Those are also the segments where cheap AI already competes.
3. **The market is not small and owned, it is crowded and unproven.** Buyers can use freelance marketplaces, contest platforms, bounty programs, or an AI model directly. Each is an alternative within reach of the same buyer. Workers must accept unpaid losing entries, and the brief names no worker-side supply plan. The two sides need each other and neither is anchored. Crypto payment and a devnet, unaudited program add friction and trust risk for non-crypto buyers.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one small market do you own first, and why can no one else serve it? Name the buyer, the task type and the count.
- What is the secret you know that others do not?
- How will the first 100 buyers arrive, and what does each cost? Is any channel repeatable?
- Of the 7 positive replies, how many will post a funded task, and at what reward? Open: how many have?
- What stops a fork with a marketing budget? Which asset is not copyable?
- Why would a worker keep entering after losses? Open: what is the expected earnings per entry?
- What is the unit volume and take rate that makes this a business? Open: nothing in the brief gives it.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable. The vote is PASS. Revisit if the founder shows:
- At least 10 real, funded tasks from outside organisations in one narrow niche, with repeat buyers.
- A defensible asset that is not open source, such as proprietary judging data, a reputation record buyers rely on, or an exclusive supply of workers.
- A held-out, independently built benchmark confirming the judge lead.
